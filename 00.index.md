# Welcome to the Computational Science interactive book

This book was created as lecture material for Computational Science at Delft University of Technology, taught as TN23015 (formerly TN2513).
It is open to everyone who wants to learn how to use programming to solve problems in physics.
We will walk you through the most important numerical techniques you will need as a physicist, and teach you how to deploy them for your day-to-day physics challenges.

Read [About this book](./00b.about.md) for a detailed overview of the content, prerequisites, and instructions on how to use this book.

## Authors

This textbook was originally written by Gary Steele, Jeroen Kalkman, Thomas Spriggs, and Eliska Greplova at the Faculty of Applied Sciences, Delft University of Technology.

TN23015 is taught in Q2 2026–2027 by Georgios Varnavides (Imaging Physics) and Danny Lathouwers (Radiation Science and Technology).

### Author contributions

Gary Steele created the content of the chapters on Differentiation, Integration, Fourier Transforms, Ordinary Differential Equations, and Partial Differential Equations.
Jeroen Kalkman created the content of the chapters on Root Finding, Linear Algebra, and Random Numbers.
Gary Steele, Jeroen Kalkman, and Eliska Greplova refined the content of all chapters while teaching the course at Delft University of Technology.
Thomas Spriggs created the [original Jupyter Book](https://oit.tudelft.nl/Computational-Science-Interactive-Textbook/) with input from Eliska Greplova.
Georgios Varnavides and Danny Lathouwers ported the content over to Jupyter Book 2 (Myst Markdown), and refined the content of all chapters while teaching the course at Delft University of Technology.
